Historical Background

Before evaluating the proposal, it helps to understand the historical context of Zcash transaction fees and the problem shape dynamic pricing is meant to address.

The Early Days

Fees began at 10,000 zatoshi, and later shrunk to 1,000. These low, fixed fees were celebrated as a core part of onboarding and accessibility.

“I personally share my Z-addr from time to time on Twitter and I get flooded with messages from the community. It’s really a special experience. I’ve also onboarded 100+ people in this way through a simple message and a few cents in Zcash.” Forum Thread

However, low fixed fees eventually enabled sandblasting episodes that flooded the chain with action-heavy traffic and degraded wallet UX.

Enter ZIP-317 and “Actions”

In response, ZIP-317 introduced the actions abstraction, consolidating transparent inputs/outputs, Sprout JoinSplits, Sapling spends/outputs, and Orchard actions into a single accounting unit.

ZIP-317 is generally credited with ending sandblasting and remains the current fee mechanism on mainnet.

Current Day

In late 2025 and early 2026, Zcash usage and visibility increased alongside stronger market interest. That changes the risk profile: even if fees are not yet a universal blocker, they are becoming a more visible UX and policy concern.

  • Community posts have reported growing urgency around fees as activity and price rise.
  • Polling suggests fees are not yet the top issue for most users, but a meaningful minority already reports concern.
  • Edge cases with many tiny UTXOs can still produce very high shielding costs.

Goals and Principles

A successful design and implementation should:

  1. Not leak information that can be used to segment the user base.
  2. Use only already-public information.
  3. Provide a clear, consistent user experience.
  4. Incentivize miners to include legitimate transactions in blocks.
  5. Respond quickly to congestion and slowly return to normal pricing.
  6. Make griefing expensive for attackers and hard for miners to game.
  7. Remain easy to implement and deploy in policy-first phases.
  8. Avoid substantive UX degradation versus today.

This combines operational goals (reliability, anti-spam, deployability) with privacy goals (reduced leakage and understandable fee buckets).

Implementation Bias Favor policy and wallet behavior first, then harden with minimal consensus changes once proven.

Naive Solutions

Two obvious options come up first:

  1. Lower ZIP-317 marginal fees. This helps short-term cost but can re-open spam economics if market conditions reverse.
  2. Increase block size. This can be ineffective when the network is not persistently congested and introduces separate tradeoffs.

Prior Art

  • EIP-1559-style congestion controllers: good responsiveness, but frequent fee changes, immediate consensus coupling, and higher complexity.
  • Compute-unit abstractions (e.g., Solana): less aligned with Zcash’s non-programmable model, where actions already provide a workable unit.
  • Opaque surge pricing: often easier to implement, but can feel predatory and reduce user agency.
Fun fact EIP-1559 ideas trace back to Zcash research threads.

A Simple Proposal: Comparable-Based Dynamic Fees

The proposal uses recent fee comparables as a market signal and keeps pricing simple, explicit, and legible for users.

A True Fee Market

Prices should emerge from available block space and real transaction demand, while still preserving clear wallet defaults and user choice.

Median-based computation for the ZIP-317 Marginal Fee

Replace a fixed marginal fee with a median per-action fee computed from a lookback window of the most recent 50 blocks, buffered by 5 blocks to reduce tip noise.

Modeling Congestion

Treat the system as always near congestion by filling slack with synthetic transactions at a configurable floor. This allows controlled stress-testing without forcing immediate consensus rules.

Fees Go Down: Marginal Fee as Median

The median anchors a “reasonable confirmation” baseline under normal conditions and creates a robust default lane for wallets.

Powers-of-10 Bucketing

Bucket to nearest powers of ten to reduce fee entropy and improve explainability. Coarse buckets also make fee changes less noisy and less linkable.

Lookback window L = {Bh-55+1, …, Bh-5} where h is the tip height and |L| = 50.

Fees Go Up: Competition and the Fast Lane

As real demand displaces synthetic slack, the baseline naturally rises. When confidence in baseline confirmation drops, expose a temporary fast lane at a higher multiplier.

This lets users choose between cost and urgency while miners remain incentivized to include higher-value transactions first.

Activation Plan

  1. Monitoring phase: run calculations off-chain and refine parameters with live data.
  2. Policy phase: ship relay + wallet guidance without consensus enforcement.
  3. Consensus phase: enforce bounded expiry and bucket constraints if policy proves out.
