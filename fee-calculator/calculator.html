Interesting Idea #1: Difficulty as Heuristic Price Oracle

From @str4d:

Mining difficulty may serve as a rough proxy for USD price over longer windows. The relationship is intriguing but should not be treated as a direct short-term signal.

Difficulty and USD price move together in this sample (Pearson r ≈ —), but daily percent changes are only weakly correlated (r ≈ —).

Interesting Idea: Inverse Cramér-Lundberg for Live Fee Tuning

From @shieldedmark:

Cramér-Lundberg models in actuarial science describe reserve dynamics under random claims. An inverse adaptation for fees could tune transaction pricing based on mempool pressure to target a stable confirmation window.

In practice, arrivals are the claim process and block inclusion is service. Fee updates would be a control signal that keeps backlog risk bounded without constantly changing user-facing prices.

Interesting Idea: Per-Block Congestion Charging

From @nuttycom:

A simpler policy variant is to adjust a marginal floor directly as a function of recent block fullness. For example, if blocks are persistently fuller, the fee floor rises proportionally; if blocks are light, the floor falls.

This is not a full market mechanism, but it may still improve UX under heavy load while remaining easy to reason about operationally.
