The thinking behind the mechanism

This design isn't being built in a vacuum. It draws on an independent mechanism-design audit, an adversarial simulator, agent-based modeling, and academic work on miner incentives. None of it gates the rollout — the mechanism ships as a suggestion and the rollout is driven by real user behavior. This research informs the optional parameter-refinement stage: if the data says a parameter should change, these are the tools and the people that help us decide how.

Mechanism design audit

Independent review by Nicolás "nikete" Della Penna.

nikete modeled the comparable-based mechanism as a repeated game between users, relay policy, and miners, and evaluated equilibrium behavior, manipulation incentives, and spam-resilience. The overall read: "promising for a privacy coin" — it deliberately trades allocative efficiency for low fee entropy and deployability.

The audit also names the conditions under which the design's claims would fail to hold — coarse-bucketing cliff effects, underspecified fast-lane semantics, the dependence of anti-spam on the fee floor. We treat these as conditions to watch in the user-behavior data, not as gates to pass before shipping. They're the sharpest questions to ask of the parameter choices on the design page.

Adversarial simulation

Built by Mohak Nahta.

Mohak built an event-driven simulator for the comparable-median fee controller, with configurable oracles, block builders, a synthetic-demand model, and a library of adversarial strategies — burst spam, median poisoning, bucket-boundary nudging, fast-lane flapping, miner self-dealing, sybil splitting. It scores each run with a harm_ratio metric capturing the cost imposed on honest users.

We used it to run an exploratory oracle × floor parameter sweep: four mechanism variants (action-weighted-median vs. median-of-medians, at floors of 1,000 and 5,000 zats) across five adversarial scenarios. The first signal — one seed — is that at the lower floor the two oracles behave identically, while at the higher floor median-of-medians resists a miner-self-dealing strategy that action-weighted-median does not. Not a verdict; a first data point that oracle and floor are coupled.

Agent-based modeling

cadCAD framework by Miguel (Simulacra R&D).

A separate, independent modeling effort built on cadCAD — Monte Carlo simulation of mempool dynamics and miner block construction across historical Zcash fee regimes (Genesis, ZIP-313, ZIP-317). A second simulator built on different foundations is valuable precisely because cross-validation across independent implementations is stronger evidence than either alone: if both agree on how a parameter change behaves, that's worth more than one model's word.

Undercutting attacks

Claire Bao, MIT M.Eng. thesis (2024).

Bao studies undercutting attacks — when a rational miner forks the chain to capture high-fee transactions instead of extending the honest tip. Her two parameters (normalized block-size limit, and how sparsely high-fee transactions arrive) govern when undercutting becomes profitable. This is the academic frame behind the miner-self-dealing scenario in the simulator above, and a useful lens on how the fee level interacts with miner incentives.
