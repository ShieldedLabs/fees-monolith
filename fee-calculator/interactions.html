What dynamic fees do not decide

Dynamic fees set the level of fees from observed congestion. Other in-flight protocol changes change who gets paid and what spam costs. This page explains how the mechanism couples with the two changes that have specifications today — Crosslink and NSM (ZIP-235) — and notes one further direction still in research.

The separation

The dynamic fee mechanism sets the total fee level from observed congestion. Allocation of that revenue across roles — miners today, finalizers under Crosslink, possibly sync infrastructure under future work like Tachyon — is a separate policy concern. This separation is deliberate: congestion pricing can evolve independently from fee-splitting policy.

A note on Tachyon: it is privacy-preserving wallet sync infrastructure, currently in R&D, with no ZIP yet. If deployed with relay-style incentives, dynamic fees would need to allocate a portion of revenue to sync infrastructure — analogous to the Crosslink finalizer role. Specifics are pending design work, so it gets only this paragraph.

Crosslink

Hybrid PoW/PoS finality without replacing PoW consensus.

Crosslink adds a Proof-of-Stake finality layer on top of Zcash's existing Proof-of-Work chain. A sidecar daemon (crosslinkd) runs BFT consensus and produces finality certificates that explorers and wallets can display alongside the standard "confirmed" indication. PoW consensus is unchanged.

Status: on testnet, with a four-phase deployment roadmap. Phase 0 (mainnet shadowing / advisory finality) sets a permissioned roster of 10–16 operators and gates go-live on ≥99% epochs finalized within target latency for 7 days, ≥80% median quorum-online, and survival of equivocation injection.

The fee interaction is allocation-only. Crosslink introduces a new role — finalizers — that must be paid to sustain the BFT layer. Dynamic fees set the total fee level from congestion; a future ZIP defines the split between miners and finalizers. The two designs are architecturally orthogonal: fees rise and fall with demand; finality timing is independent. The size of the finalizer share will depend on how Crosslink's validator-economics parameters land.

NSM — ZIP-235

The Network Sustainability Mechanism. Burns 60% of transaction fees; pays miners 40%.

ZIP-235 is the consensus rule that splits transaction fee revenue. 60% is removed from circulation permanently; 40% is paid to the miner of the block. The 60/40 split is calibrated against a security argument: at 50/50 or any split more favorable to miners, miners gain a rollback incentive on prior blocks — the burn portion benefits them only proportional to future hashrate share, while the direct miner share is 100% theirs. The 60/40 split keeps the direct share strictly less than the future-share of the burn, removing the rollback incentive.

The interaction with dynamic fees is amplification. As the Layer 3 estimator raises the fee level during sustained congestion, the burn amount scales proportionally. An attacker mounting sustained spam pays not just the base fee but a burn cost that grows with the level they've forced the estimator to publish. This is the "teeth" of the design: the mechanism doesn't merely price congestion, it makes inducing congestion permanently expensive.

Marginal fee coupling. A separate draft ZIP proposes reducing the ZIP-317 marginal_fee from 5,000 zats to 1,000 zats to compensate for ZEC price appreciation. The dynamic estimator uses the marginal fee as its floor; ZIP-235 sets the burn percentage on top. The two parameters must remain compatible — specifically, the minimum fee at any action count must remain divisible by the 60/40 split.
